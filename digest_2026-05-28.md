# nnU-Net Part II: Training Protocol, Inference Strategy, and What Automated Baselines Revealed About the Field

**Date:** 2026-05-28 | **Arc:** 2/2 — nnU-Net and the U-Net family

Yesterday's session established how nnU-Net converts a dataset fingerprint into a fully specified pipeline — determining patch size, resampling targets, normalization strategy, and which of three architectural configurations to instantiate from the geometry and statistics of the training data alone. What remained implicit was the training and inference machinery that runs inside each configuration once it has been configured. Understanding those details matters because several of nnU-Net's most important design choices occur at the level of optimization, augmentation, and inference aggregation rather than at the level of architecture, and because those choices explain a substantial portion of the empirical success that made nnU-Net the de facto benchmark baseline across the field.

The loss function combines two terms: a soft Dice loss computed per class and then averaged across classes, and a standard cross-entropy loss weighted by inverse class frequency. Soft Dice directly optimizes the overlap coefficient between predicted and ground-truth segmentation maps, which makes it implicitly robust to the extreme foreground-to-background imbalance endemic to medical segmentation. A small hepatic lesion might occupy fewer than 0.1% of voxels in an abdominal CT volume; cross-entropy treated naively would produce a degenerate network that never predicts the foreground class and yet achieves 99.9% pixel accuracy. The Dice loss circumvents this pathology because its numerator and denominator are computed only within the union of the predicted and ground-truth foreground masks, making it invariant to the absolute count of background voxels. The soft version avoids the non-differentiability of the hard binary Dice by replacing the indicator function with the predicted probability, which allows gradients to flow cleanly through the thresholding-free computation graph. The combination with cross-entropy is empirically motivated: cross-entropy provides smoother and more informative gradients early in training when the predicted probabilities are near uniform, while Dice becomes progressively more informative once the network has learned approximate localization and the class distributions are no longer degenerate.

Deep supervision is applied throughout the training process by attaching auxiliary loss branches to each level of the decoder. The contribution of coarser-resolution branches is downweighted by a geometric factor — halved at each level away from the finest output — so that the total loss remains dominated by the highest-resolution prediction. The gradient signal through deeper decoder layers is therefore not impoverished relative to the primary loss, which accelerates convergence in early epochs when the encoder has not yet learned useful representations. Without deep supervision the lower decoder levels are effectively trained only through the gradient path from the final output, which in a deep network can be attenuated enough to slow learning substantially.

The data augmentation pipeline is deliberately aggressive and constitutes a significant portion of nnU-Net's engineering contribution. On-the-fly augmentation during each training iteration includes random rotations in all three axes for volumetric data or in the image plane for the 2D configuration, random isotropic and anisotropic scaling, additive Gaussian noise, Gaussian blurring with randomly sampled kernel sizes, brightness and contrast shifts, simulation of low-resolution artifacts through deliberate downsampling and upsampling, random gamma corrections applied to the intensity histogram, and random mirroring along each axis. Each of these operations is applied stochastically with independently tuned probabilities. The motivation for such a broad augmentation space is that medical imaging datasets are consistently small by the standards of natural image benchmarks — dozens to hundreds of annotated volumes rather than hundreds of thousands of labeled examples — and the high-dimensional implicit regularization from strong augmentation compensates for this scarcity. The mirroring augmentation deserves particular note: anatomical structures are approximately bilaterally symmetric in most modalities, so training with both orientations effectively presents the network with a symmetric distribution of left-right configurations, which is especially beneficial for paired structures such as kidneys, lungs, and femoral heads that must be distinguished by context rather than intrinsic appearance.

At inference time, nnU-Net applies sliding window inference with Gaussian-weighted patch aggregation. The input volume is covered by a grid of overlapping patches matching the patch size used during training, and the network produces a class probability map for each patch. Where patches overlap, predictions are aggregated via a weighted average in which the weight at each voxel is given by a Gaussian function centered at the patch midpoint and falling toward the edges. This weighting suppresses the contribution of predictions near patch boundaries, which tend to be less reliable because the network's effective receptive field is truncated near the image boundary — there are fewer context voxels on one side. Uniform averaging at boundaries would systematically degrade the quality of the final segmentation along patch seams; Gaussian weighting eliminates this artifact with minimal computational overhead. The sliding window stride is set as a fixed fraction of the patch size to ensure that the overlap is sufficient for the Gaussian weighting to smooth adequately across patch transitions.

Test-time augmentation is applied by default when generating final predictions. Predictions are computed for all axis-aligned mirror combinations of the input — eight forward passes for 3D data — and the resulting probability maps are averaged before the argmax is taken to obtain the discrete label map. The computational cost is therefore multiplied by a factor of eight relative to single-pass inference, but the variance reduction from averaging over mirror-symmetric views consistently improves the Dice coefficient by one to two percentage points on held-out test sets. This is not a negligible improvement in a field where differences between competing methods often fall within that range.

Post-processing decisions are likewise automated. The most consequential is connected component filtering applied per class: nnU-Net examines the training annotations to determine whether each target class always appears as a single connected component, then evaluates on cross-validation data whether removing small spurious components actually improves performance, and applies the rule at inference time only if it provides measurable benefit. This conditional post-processing is subtle but important — it avoids the false positive from leaving a small spurious component in the prediction and equally avoids the false negative from over-aggressively suppressing foreground in tasks where multiple disconnected components are anatomically expected, such as individual vertebrae or metastatic lesions. The rule is derived from the data, not encoded by hand.

The broader intellectual legacy of nnU-Net is perhaps more significant than any of its individual technical choices. When evaluated on the Medical Segmentation Decathlon — twenty-three heterogeneous tasks spanning hepatic vessels, brain tumors, pancreas, cardiac substructures, and colon polyps — the self-configured system with no human intervention per dataset matched or exceeded the performance of manually tuned task-specific methods on the majority of tasks. This result forced a difficult conversation about attribution. If a consistently engineered baseline achieves top performance, how much of the apparent progress in published architectures is attributable to genuine architectural innovation versus inadequately optimized prior baselines? The uncomfortable answer is that a substantial fraction of reported gains in the pre-nnU-Net literature reflect baseline miscalibration rather than architectural superiority. nnU-Net did not merely establish a strong starting point; it changed the evidentiary standard for claiming that a new architecture represents a real improvement. Any proposed extension of U-Net must now demonstrate gains over a properly configured nnU-Net instantiation rather than over a casually tuned baseline, which is a substantially more demanding test.

This epistemological point carries directly into the next arc of the curriculum: the 3D U-Net, Attention U-Net, and the hybrid transformer variants TransUNet and Swin-UNet. Each was designed to address a specific limitation of the vanilla U-Net — limited volumetric context, spatially diffuse skip connection features, or the absence of long-range relational structure in the encoder. Whether these architectural changes provide improvements that survive rigorous comparison against a well-configured nnU-Net is a question worth holding in mind as we examine each modification in turn.
